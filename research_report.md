**1. Introduction**

Workforce shortages among healthcare professionals in Nigeria have been widely reported, impacting service delivery, patient outcomes, and health system efficiency. Artificial Intelligence (AI)–powered diagnostic tools are increasingly discussed as a potential strategy to alleviate these shortages by improving diagnostic speed, accuracy, and accessibility. This report evaluates the evidence supplied to determine *in what ways* AI can mitigate these workforce shortages.

**2. Key Findings**

| Finding | Evidence Classification | Research Relevance | Source Type | Citation |

- No direct measured outcomes or studies were provided that link AI use to a reduction in workforce shortages in Nigerian healthcare settings.

**3. Interpretation of Evidence**

The first finding, although sourced from an organizational website, suggests that AI diagnostic tools can improve diagnostic speed and accuracy, which could, in theory, reduce the demand placed on limited specialist staff. However, the evidence is descriptive and does not quantify workload reductions, staff time saved, or changes in staffing requirements.

The second finding documents a trend in scholarly output on AI, reflecting increased research activity and potential technological readiness. Yet, publication volume alone does not indicate real‑world implementation or impact on workforce dynamics.

Both pieces of evidence are *background* in nature and *indirect* or *contextual* relative to the research question. They provide a conceptual basis for considering AI as a possible workforce mitigation tool but do not empirically substantiate that role in Nigeria.

**4. Limitations**

- **Evidence Scope**: Only two background‐context findings were supplied; none directly address workforce shortages.
- **Data Type**: No measured outcomes (e.g., reduced clinician workload, patient throughput, or staffing ratios) are reported.
- **Source Provenance**: One source is an organizational website; the other is a peer‑reviewed journal article but reports on publication trends, not implementation outcomes.
- **Geographical Focus**: The first source claims applicability “in Nigeria” but lacks specific empirical data; the second source does not specify a Nigerian context.
- **Inference Gap**: While faster, more accurate, and accessible diagnosis *could* alleviate workforce pressure, causality or magnitude cannot be inferred from the provided evidence.

**5. Conclusion**

Current evidence does **not** establish concrete ways in which AI mitigates workforce shortages among healthcare professionals in Nigeria. The available findings suggest potential benefits of AI diagnostics in terms of speed, accuracy, and accessibility, and indicate growing research interest in AI. However, without direct empirical studies measuring workforce outcomes, the role of AI in reducing staffing deficits remains unverified. Further research—particularly implementation studies with quantitative outcomes—is required to substantiate AI’s impact on workforce shortages in Nigerian healthcare settings.
